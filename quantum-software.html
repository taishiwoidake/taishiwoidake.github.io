Can we trust
the compiler?

✦

This independent research interest concerns quantum compiler testing: whether gate rewrites, optimisation passes and circuit transformations preserve the semantics they promise, including rare edge cases.

EXPLORE THE RESEARCH ↗
RESEARCH NOTES

What compiler correctness means

A quantum compiler transforms a source program into operations that can be run on a target system. A correctness claim must identify the semantics preserved by the transformation: for example a unitary operation, a quantum channel or the distribution of specified measurement outcomes.

A transformation can be correct under one notion of equivalence and incorrect under another. The research direction focuses on explicit contracts and reproducible counterexamples rather than treating every change in circuit syntax as a defect.

A harmless
simplification?

An ordinary compiler might simplify an expression that happens to fail for a rare negative input. Quantum software transformations also need edge-case tests.

INTUITIVE PICTURE

The everyday version

If a calculator shortcut passes familiar inputs but fails for one unusual sign or boundary case, the shortcut is not a correct replacement.

SCIENTIFIC QUESTION

The quantum version

For example, two consecutive ideal Hadamard gates equal the identity, so removing them is a valid ideal-unitary rewrite. For a general transformation, the exact contract may instead concern a distribution, observable or channel.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM · An observed simulation difference is not sufficient without a valid oracle and the transformation's documented contract.
RESEARCH NOTES

Equivalent circuits need not look alike

Two unitary matrices differing only by a global phase describe the same isolated quantum channel. However, replacing an operation inside a controlled construction may require more care: a phase that was global for the isolated operation can become relative in the larger circuit.

A test oracle must therefore match the context in which the compiler pass promises equivalence. Comparing raw matrix entries without considering the contract can yield false reports.

RESEARCH NOTES

From a failure to a minimal example

Construct a legal input circuit, record the compiler and dependency versions, and apply the relevant transformation under controlled settings. Compare the outputs using an oracle appropriate to the promised semantics.

A useful reproducer isolates the smallest failure that still exhibits the discrepancy. It should retain enough information for another researcher to repeat the test without depending on hidden settings or undocumented state.

What makes
a bug real?

A strong report isolates the triggering input and distinguishes a real rewrite error from a bad test assumption.

REPRODUCE

Small legal input

Reduce the failing circuit to a small legal input and record its gate sequence, parameters, pass pipeline, tool version, expected semantics and actual output.

VERIFY

Correct semantics

For ideal-unitary equality up to global phase, compare U and V using a phase-invariant criterion, such as whether |tr(U†V)| equals the Hilbert-space dimension. A different contract requires a different oracle.

EXPLAIN

Root cause and scope

Use differential testing, metamorphic relations or exact reference calculations to isolate the failure. Distinguish a suspected defect, a reproducible violation and a confirmed upstream issue.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM · This is a software-verification line, not a theorem proven from QTRAC or Ximeste–Maluna.
RESEARCH NOTES

Numerical and logical discrepancies

For small ideal circuits, a unitary comparison up to global phase or a channel comparison can be a useful reference. For larger or noisy circuits, statistical or approximate tests require declared tolerances and assumptions.

A difference larger than a numerical threshold is not automatically a software bug. The input must be legal, the transformation's contract must be understood, and the discrepancy must survive independent checking.

RESEARCH NOTES

Evidence rather than a vulnerability count

The aim is to characterise conditions under which a transformation fails to preserve its promised meaning, not to maximise the number of reported failures. Reproducibility and careful negative controls are part of the result.

This page describes a research methodology and does not claim that a particular compiler defect has been confirmed.
