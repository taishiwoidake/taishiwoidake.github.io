RESEARCH NOTES

Finite-state transformations and temporal compatibility

Ximeste–Maluna investigates the distinction between a family of locally feasible operations and a single admissible realisation across multiple stages. Each local stage may appear possible in isolation without there being a common sequence that satisfies all the constraints together.

The programme asks how this distinction depends on state-space size n, permitted operation arity q and the horizon of the task. A classification is meaningful only after the allowed local maps and the required global behaviour have been fixed.

✳ 01 / FINITE TEMPORAL STRUCTURE ✳

When size
meets time.

A central question is whether a family of locally feasible transformations can be realised together as a single temporally compatible sequence. Ximeste–Maluna studies finite-state transport and the effect of operation arity q and state-space size n.

ARITY q

Local capabilities

Arity describes how many components a permitted local move can involve. A change in allowed operations can change the classification even at the same state-space size.

STATE SIZE n

Global structure

The integer n counts the underlying finite states. Any auxiliary memory or joint state space must be included in the precise model rather than silently treated as free.

TEMPORAL ORDER

Sequential constraints

A transformation may be individually realisable at each time while the proposed realisations fail to form one common trajectory. The question is whether there is a joint witness.

STRUCTURAL PARAMETERS(n, q)An indexing pair for a restricted problem family, not a claimed universal formula.

The exact operation family and critical notion must be specified before stating a theorem.

RESEARCH NOTES

A mathematical way to describe compatibility

For each stage t, consider the relation Rₜ of pairs of states connected by an allowed operation. A path through the relations must satisfy the matching intermediate-state constraints. Knowing that each Rₜ is nonempty does not guarantee the existence of a complete path with prescribed endpoints.

This elementary observation motivates more restrictive finite transport questions. It is not itself a proof of the proposed critical curve. Additional constraints on shared transformations, local moves and witnesses determine the actual problem.

✳ 02 / CAPACITY AND OBSTRUCTION ✳

What can
be attained?

Finite constructions are used to prove achievability in specified cases, while combinatorial or arithmetic certificates rule out classes of witnesses. The critical-curve question seeks a sharp boundary between these two outcomes.

CONSTRUCTION

Lower witnesses

A construction must specify every allowed operation and show that its composition has the required temporal property.

OBSTRUCTION

Upper limitations

A certificate identifies an invariant or counting obstruction that every admissible sequence obeys, regardless of the search algorithm used.

DUALITY

Matching frontier

A sharp classification needs matching constructions and obstructions, including exceptional parameter pairs. The general critical-curve conjecture remains open.

Sharpness claims require complete assumptions, not merely finite computational examples.

RESEARCH NOTES

Constructive and obstruction certificates

A constructive witness provides a finite sequence of admissible operations together with the verification of its endpoint and intermediate constraints. An obstruction may come from an invariant, an exact count, an inequality or an exhaustive certificate for a completely specified finite instance.

A sharp result needs a clear account of which side of the boundary the witness establishes, and which other parameter pairs are excluded. A real-valued relaxation can help find bounds, but an integral realisation requires its own argument.

RESEARCH NOTES

Why synthesis enters the programme

A stochastic map describes transition probabilities rather than a deterministic final state. Repeated composition of a stochastic map changes the exact arithmetic of those probabilities. The implementation cost depends on the permitted gates, auxiliary resources and whether a fixed-depth or stopping-time model is used.

This is related to finite operational limitations but is not automatically equivalent to a temporal compatibility problem. A claimed equivalence would require a resource-preserving reduction in both directions.

RESEARCH NOTES

Research status and verification

The arity-by-state-size classification is an open research direction on this website. The public technical-v1 manuscript concerns specified regular-cover and stopping-tree models and does not, by itself, resolve that classification.

For a future general statement, the required evidence includes the exact model, a precise theorem, proofs covering all stated parameter regimes and explicit treatment of exceptional cases. Public finite certificates should remain traceable to the corresponding claims.
