RESEARCH NOTES

Begin with a simple question

Imagine trying to learn which of two events happened yesterday. You do not need to reconstruct every detail of yesterday to answer that question; you need evidence that differs between the two possibilities.

The research pages turn this intuitive problem into mathematical tasks. Some focus on quantum states, others on finite transformations, and others on physical or empirical observations.

✳   2 / START HERE   ✳

A note from
yesterday.

Imagine something happens in a closed room. Tomorrow, you may inspect only one small box taken from that room.

IN EVERYDAY LANGUAGE

The everyday version

If the box looks exactly the same after two different possible events, the box by itself cannot tell you which event occurred. We do not get missing evidence simply by wishing for it.

IN THE MATHEMATICAL MODEL

The research version

Suppose either event is equally likely. If both leave exactly the same accessible state, the best possible decision succeeds only half the time. If their accessible records are perfectly distinguishable, the event can be identified with certainty.

THE LIMIT · The target is to verify a specified event or alternative, not to claim unlimited recovery of every lost detail.
RESEARCH NOTES

When evidence is not accessible

Suppose two different events leave identical notes in the only box you can inspect. No clever reading method can distinguish the events using those notes alone. Another box may contain evidence, but it is useful only if your rules permit access.

This is why each research question specifies the observer's resources. More powerful reasoning cannot substitute for a missing distinction in the permitted evidence.

Record, memory
and test.

Quantum Temporal Reconstruction examines what can still be tested about a past event when access, storage and time are limited.

01 / RECORD

Evidence

An event can change the state of a physical system, but an observer may not control every part of it.

02 / MEMORY

Keep a clue

Imagine being allowed to retain one bit from a two-bit message before learning which bit will be requested. Storing everything is impossible, so the encoding must be designed for the later task.

03 / ANSWER

A future test

The observer produces an answer. Across repeated trials, the fraction of correct answers measures performance, and a mathematical upper bound tells us how far any allowed strategy can go.

THE LIMIT · A quantum memory is not a magical recording device. Any advantage over classical memory depends on the task.
RESEARCH NOTES

Three key mathematical ideas

A model states the possible events and what information is accessible. A protocol states how to process that information. A performance measure states how well the protocol answers the question.

An achievable result proves that a protocol works. An impossibility bound proves that no permitted protocol can do better than a specified value. Combining the two can determine an optimal resource requirement.

RESEARCH NOTES

Choose the branch that matches your interests

Readers interested in quantum theory can begin with QTRAC and then explore finite memory, access order and delayed questions. Readers interested in discrete mathematics can follow Ximeste–Maluna and exact probabilistic circuits.

Readers interested in physical experiments can explore thermal records and directional interference. Quantum software and mobility analysis are separate applied research directions.
