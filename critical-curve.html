The geometry
of limits.

Ximeste–Maluna studies how finite-horizon transport and local operation restrictions determine whether separately feasible stages admit a common time-compatible realisation. The arity-by-state-size critical-curve question asks how this feasibility boundary varies with q and n.

Small local
moves.

Consider rearranging a finite system when each allowed move may involve only a limited number of positions.

EVERYDAY EXAMPLE

An everyday analogy

Suppose several arrangements are reachable from a starting board. Knowing each arrangement is reachable separately does not show that one legal sequence can pass through them in the required order. A restriction on the moves can turn separate feasibility into a compatibility problem.

FORMAL RESEARCH

Mathematical framing

Let n count underlying states and q describe the allowed local operation arity. For each specified family, a feasibility test asks whether the same permitted joint evolution can realise all required stages.

IMPORTANT · The pair (n,q) alone is not a theorem. The admissible transformations and property must also be defined.

Finite checks
are not enough.

A mathematical programme must resolve exceptional configurations and prove that finite examples extend, or clearly label them as exceptions.

CONSTRUCTIVE

Give a witness

Give explicit states, legal operations and intermediate configurations. Check that one common trajectory satisfies every specified constraint.

DUAL CERTIFICATE

Prove a limit

Find a counting invariant, transport restriction or dual certificate that applies to all admissible witnesses for a stated parameter pair.

UNRESOLVED CASES

Close the gaps

Test exceptional values, boundary cases and changes in allowable memory. A complete classification needs an argument excluding unexplored counterexamples.

IMPORTANT · The general critical-curve classification is explicitly OPEN; it should not be marketed as a completed universal theorem.
