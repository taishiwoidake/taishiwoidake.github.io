✳ 01 / THE QUESTION ✳

Evidence after
the event.

Imagine a device that was prepared in one of two ways yesterday. Today its environment is only partly accessible. QTRAC asks which earlier preparation can still be identified, using a specified sequence of operations and a limited memory.

PAST ALTERNATIVE

What happened?

Choose hypotheses x = 0 or 1, their prior probabilities and the corresponding initial states. The question is which hypothesis produced the available evidence.

ACCESSIBLE INFORMATION

What remains?

Evolution can distribute distinguishing information across several systems. The verifier might see each system only once, or never have access to some systems.

FUTURE VERIFICATION

What can be tested?

Choose sequential encodings and a final measurement to maximise the probability of a correct answer, while respecting the memory and access constraints.

For two equally likely alternatives with identical accessible states, every measurement on those states has success probability 1/2. Identifying an event is not equivalent to reconstructing a microscopic history.

✳ 03 / THE RESOURCE FRONTIER ✳

The order
matters.

Timing changes the resources available to verify the past. The central goal is a precise separation or bound, not an unrestricted reconstruction claim.

ACCESS ORDER

Early or late?

In sequential-access models, interacting with one part before another can force a record to be stored for longer. The research compares different contact orders under the same physical constraints.

FINITE MEMORY

How much storage?

Study how much classical or coherent quantum storage is necessary to distinguish specified histories. Lower bounds must apply to every admissible strategy, not just a particular circuit.

UNKNOWN TIME

When will we test?

A future test may arrive at an unknown time or select an unknown question. A memory optimal for one known test need not work for a family of later tests.

Explore the precise tasks in the linked memory, observation-order and delayed-question branches. The broader programme also investigates finite-state compatibility and reversible hidden-memory control.
