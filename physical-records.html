✳ 02 / CONTROLLED RETURN ✳

What does
reversal reveal?

A carefully designed echo or reversal sequence can define a test of what remains accessible after a controlled evolution.

FORWARD PROCESS

Create alternatives

Use matched initial conditions and writing alternatives so that differences in the final signal can be attributed to the stated interaction.

REVERSE OPERATION

Attempt return

An ideal inverse evolution undoes a known unitary. A real echo may reverse only part of the dynamics; leakage, uncontrolled couplings and errors must be included.

OBSERVABLE

Test interference

Read out interference contrast or the trace distance between accessible output states. An observable must be linked to the proposed model by a derivation.

Uncontrolled environments and noisy operations prevent any blanket claim of perfect information recovery.

✳ 03 / DIRECTIONAL INVERSE PROBLEMS ✳

The hidden
spatial kernel.

The inverse-problem direction asks whether parallel and transverse interference responses can distinguish interaction kernels whose direction-averaged signals coincide.

ORIENTATION

Retain angular data

The Laplacian averages directional curvature through the Hessian trace; individual directional derivatives can retain information lost in that sum.

MODEL

Specify the physics

For example, ∇²(1/r) = 0 away from the source, although the Hessian of 1/r is nonzero. This motivates comparing directional observables.

INVERSION

Test identifiability

Test uniqueness, noise sensitivity and nuisance parameters before interpreting a directional signal as evidence for one interaction model.

STANDARD MATHEMATICAL EXAMPLE∇²V = tr(HV) ;   directional curvature = uᵀHVuThe trace of a Hessian and its curvature along a unit direction u are different observables. A physical protocol must establish how its measured signal depends on them.

A theoretical directional signature is not a detection of a new force or physical law.
