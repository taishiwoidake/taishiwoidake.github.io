RESEARCH NOTES

A programme about accessible evidence

My research examines the limits of inference in systems with finite resources. The central quantum-information question is how much a later observer can establish about an earlier event when evidence is distributed across systems that cannot all be accessed at once.

The mathematical and physical branches are organised around related questions about finite constraints, compatibility and measurable traces. Their definitions and proof obligations remain distinct.

Imagine two different preparations of a quantum system. As time passes, evidence may spread into an inaccessible environment. A later observer can inspect only certain registers and retain limited memory. Can the two earlier preparations still be distinguished?

The goal is not to assume that an entire past can be recovered, but to establish operational limits under clearly specified assumptions.

RESEARCH NOTES

Distinguishing histories

An operational question begins with competing hypotheses and the evidence each would produce. A successful verifier distinguishes the hypotheses better than a suitable baseline. If the evidence is identical under the allowed observations, no decision rule can extract the missing distinction.

This perspective avoids confusing a recoverable record with full microscopic reconstruction. It also clarifies why the limits of a particular observer need not coincide with the limits of a hypothetical observer with unrestricted access.

RESEARCH NOTES

From a question to a mathematical model

Specify the allowed inputs, evolution or transformations, retained state and permitted observations. Fix an objective such as guessing success, implementation depth or expected cost. A protocol gives an achievable value; a bound constrains every protocol in the same model.

The strongest classifications match these two sides under explicit assumptions. When they do not match, the difference gives a precise open problem rather than a reason to claim a universal result.

RESEARCH NOTES

How the research directions fit together

QTRAC examines temporal verification under constrained quantum information. Ximeste–Maluna and probabilistic circuits concern finite mathematical structures and implementation limits. Physical-record research examines distinguishability and controlled reversal in specified physical systems.

Quantum software verification and human mobility are separate applied directions. Their shared concern is reliable inference under a declared model, not a claim that one theorem solves all of them.
