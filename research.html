01THE CENTRAL QUESTION

WHERE THE RESEARCH BEGINS

What remains
knowable?

A measurement is made. Time passes. Some information remains accessible; some has flowed into an environment we cannot examine.

Now imagine a second observer arriving later. With only limited memory and a restricted set of operations, how reliably can they verify what happened before?

My research interests centre on formalising such temporal verification tasks: which past events can be distinguished, when they can be checked, and what resources must be retained to do so.

✳

The distinction matters. Reconstructing an entire physical past is generally not possible from incomplete information. The mathematical problem is to identify precisely which claims about the past remain testable under stated assumptions.

SCHEMATIC OBJECTIVE

For a binary past alternative x, consider the best achievable guessing probability under a memory bound m, deadline T, and a declared class of admissible strategies.

Pguess*(m,T) = supadmissible protocols Pr[ x̂ = x ]
This is a generic optimisation objective, not a theorem or a claimed numerical result. Priors and access rules must be specified.

04MAKING THE CLAIM PRECISE

OPEN QUESTIONS

The details
change everything.

Careful assumptions decide whether a reconstruction claim is meaningful, impossible, or achievable.

What is being verified?＋

A particular classical event, an intervention, or a set of distinguishable histories must be defined. Recovering a label is different from reconstructing an entire trajectory.

What can the observer access?＋

The accessible system, inaccessible environment, initial-state assumptions, allowed instruments, and whether adaptive operations are permitted all affect the task.

What counts as memory?＋

Classical and quantum memory should be counted separately where relevant. A memory bound has meaning only after specifying which registers and auxiliary systems are included.

How is success evaluated?＋

Verification can be formulated through hypothesis-testing error, guessing probability, or another operational figure of merit, with an explicit prior and deadline.
