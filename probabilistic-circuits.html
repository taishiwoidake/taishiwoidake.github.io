Fair coins.
Exact maps.

A fair binary random choice is simple, but a circuit combining many choices can implement a nontrivial stochastic transformation. My question is how exactness, circuit depth, iteration and finite auxiliary memory constrain this implementation.

What does
a fair coin build?

Two independent fair tosses give four equally likely outcomes, letting us group them into probabilities with denominator four.

EVERYDAY EXAMPLE

A simple calculation

Two fair bits yield 00, 01, 10 and 11 with equal probability. Declaring success on the first three outcomes gives exactly 3/4. More generally, an unrestricted deterministic decision on t independent fair bits has probability k/2ᵗ.

FORMAL RESEARCH

An exact synthesis task

A stochastic matrix assigns a probability distribution over output states to each input. Synthesis asks for a single circuit realising every row while respecting its allowed gate set, randomness and reusable memory.

IMPORTANT · A matrix having dyadic entries does not automatically settle minimal implementation depth under a restricted gate set.

What is
being minimised?

A valid bound must specify what gates are free, which auxiliary registers are allowed, the error metric and the relevant matrix family.

EXACT

Entry-by-entry equality

Every entry of the implemented transition matrix must equal the target rational number. A denominator obstruction can matter even when the numerical approximation would look excellent.

APPROXIMATE

A defined tolerance

An approximate circuit may differ from its target by at most ε in a chosen metric, such as worst-case total variation distance across input rows.

RESOURCE

Memory and depth

A depth lower bound must quantify the allowed gates and helper memory. Counting fair choices gives a useful baseline, but it need not prove the best bound for every circuit architecture.

IMPORTANT · Special conditional depth formulae are not universal synthesis theorems.
