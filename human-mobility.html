A crowd is
not an answer.

A busy railway station can reflect work, school, weather, delays and special events. Seeing a crowd does not identify its cause.

INTUITIVE PICTURE

An everyday observation

A crowded platform at 8 a.m. is one observation, not a complete explanation of why everyone is there.

SCIENTIFIC QUESTION

A research question

An empirical analysis begins by defining who or what is observed, how records are collected, their temporal and spatial resolution, and what competing explanations would predict.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM · Predicting observed movement patterns is not equivalent to establishing causal explanations.

✳   4 / HUMAN MOBILITY   ✳

Which patterns
are stable?

Reasonable research questions include temporal regularities, network flows and uncertainty induced by limited data.

TIME

Daily variation

Compare hourly and daily patterns using matched observation windows. Separate periodic variation from unusual events and collection artefacts.

SPACE

Origins and destinations

Represent regions as vertices and aggregated flows as weighted edges. Study network structure only at a suitable privacy-preserving resolution.

UNCERTAINTY

What is identifiable?

Check sensitivity to aggregation, missing observations and model choices. A change in recorded movement is not automatically a change in population behaviour.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM · This page describes an interest and ongoing research direction, not a claim of a completed mobility publication.

A different field.
Shared care.

Both mobility analysis and temporal verification motivate careful inference from limited records, but their data models, estimators and evidence standards differ. This is a separate research direction, not an asserted application of a QTRAC theorem.
