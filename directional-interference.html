✳   2 / DIRECTIONAL INFERENCE   ✳

The average
can hide a shape.

Two objects can have the same average width while looking very different when examined from different directions.

INTUITIVE PICTURE

The simple picture

A long thin object and a broad round object can share an average measurement even though their directional dimensions differ.

SCIENTIFIC QUESTION

The inverse problem

Prepare and control the geometry of an interferometric protocol; compare measurements under different orientations and test whether the source kernel is identifiable.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM · A directional pattern is not, on its own, evidence of a new gravitational force.

✳   4 / DIRECTIONAL INFERENCE   ✳

How would
we measure it?

The research question is to derive a physically measurable contrast from a controlled forward-and-reverse sequence, then determine whether its directional dependence constrains the underlying kernel.

CONTROL

Define two paths

Specify the geometry, separation, coupling, state preparation, forward and reverse dynamics. Hold nuisance parameters fixed when comparing orientations.

OBSERVABLE

Measure visibility

Use a precisely defined visibility observable, and propagate its measurement uncertainty. Any logarithmic visibility contrast requires positive values and a justified model relating it to the kernel.

IDENTIFIABILITY

Compare explanations

An inverse problem requires more than different predictions at one parameter value. Test identifiability against parameter degeneracies, measurement error and alternative models.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM · No experiment has here been shown to reconstruct a spatial kernel or discover a new physical law.
