RESEARCH NOTES

Order as a resource

The order of observations determines which correlations can be combined before an earlier register is lost. Even if two schedules visit the same registers, their sets of feasible encoding strategies can differ when memory is restricted.

If every register can be stored without cost until a common final measurement, many ordering differences disappear. The finite-memory restriction is what makes the comparison interesting.

THE RESEARCH MODEL

The quantum model

Fix an identical family of states and operations, but change the permitted order of contacts. Compare the resource cost of each protocol fairly.

SCOPE · A valid order effect requires matching the underlying model, not comparing entirely different physical experiments.
RESEARCH NOTES

A simple classical illustration

Consider a sequence of values followed by an index specifying which value matters. If the index arrives first, the observer need only retain the requested value. If it arrives last, the observer must encode the sequence before knowing which value will be needed.

This example separates the timing of information from its total amount. It is an illustration of delayed selection, not a claimed new theorem about all possible quantum contact orders.

RESEARCH NOTES

Correlations that are locally invisible

For the Bell states (|00⟩ ± |11⟩)/√2, each single-qubit reduced density matrix is I/2. Measuring either qubit alone cannot reveal the sign. A joint measurement can distinguish the two orthogonal global states perfectly.

A sequential protocol must specify whether the earlier qubit can be kept coherently or whether only a classical result may survive. For these particular Bell states, appropriately coordinated local measurements can also reveal the sign, so the example alone is not a proof of a quantum-memory advantage.

✳   4 / OBSERVATION ORDER   ✳

Why proving
it is hard.

An ordering claim requires both a protocol that works and a limitation applying to every other allowed protocol.

LOWER BOUND

Exclude all smaller memories

If the first half is accessible only before the second, a proposed lower bound must cover all allowed encodings of the first contact into the retained register, including collective strategies where permitted.

ACHIEVABILITY

Build the storage method

In the illustrative Bell-pair task, keeping the first qubit until the second arrives allows a Bell-basis measurement, provided joint measurement is permitted. This is a concrete achievable protocol, not a general lower bound.

MATCHING CONDITIONS

Hold other factors fixed

A fair comparison varies the contact sequence while holding the label prior, physical states, available operations, memory accounting and desired success probability fixed.

SCOPE · Finite constructions are informative, but a general classification requires stronger proof.
RESEARCH NOTES

What constitutes an order separation

Define the same hypothesis ensemble and performance criterion for both schedules. Account for all retained registers, classical outcomes and fresh access. Exhibit an admissible protocol for one schedule, then bound the optimum for the other.

A comparison of two selected circuits is insufficient: a different encoding may outperform either circuit. An impossibility claim requires a bound over the whole declared strategy space.

RESEARCH NOTES

Timing and the larger QTRAC problem

The operational role of order is clearest when information needed later appears before the question or before a complementary register becomes available. The relevant memory may have to retain correlations rather than a completed answer.

The finite-memory and delayed-question branches develop the complementary constraints. The long-term goal is a classification of tasks where changing the order changes the optimal resource requirement.
